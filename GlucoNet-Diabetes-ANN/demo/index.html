04 / METHOD

How the neural network works

04A / PREPROCESSING

Data, without leakage

Impossible zeros in glucose, blood pressure, skinfold, insulin, and BMI are recoded as missing. Pregnancies of zero are left as a real value. Medians and z-scores are learned on the training fold only. The minority class is oversampled inside that fold; the scored row is never copied.

04B / TOPOLOGY

A small feed-forward net

  • Input layer: 8 biomarkers
  • Hidden: 16 then 8 units, ReLU
  • Output: 1 unit, logistic sigmoid
  • Adam, L2 of 10⁻³, early stopping

Two hidden layers are enough for this table. A deeper net did not improve ranking in the ablation study.

05A / READING THE BARS

Clinical context still comes first

The bars are the drop in hold-out ROC-AUC when a column is shuffled. Glucose carries most of the signal, then age, BMI, and pregnancies — the same order a clinic would expect for type 2 risk.

That agreement is useful, and it is also a warning. A network that leans almost entirely on one lab value is not discovering a hidden pattern. It is restating a known relationship on a small, historical table. A physician still has to place any score next to symptoms, family history, and a proper diagnostic test.

This tool is for education and screening-style illustration. It does not diagnose diabetes, does not replace fasting glucose, OGTT, or HbA1c, and should not be used to make treatment decisions. The underlying sample is adult women from one community, examined decades ago. Do not apply the score to men, children, or other populations.
